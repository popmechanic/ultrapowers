# Jev reads the reviewer's leftovers, and the card puts the few to act on at the top

**Grammar:** claims-v1

**Claim:** The PR card gains one section, "Act on these", listing rows at attention ≥ 2.5, capped at ten; the full list stays below it unchanged. Nothing gates on it. (quoted from #1093)
**Summary:** The card files every reviewer leftover among a thousand nits, and its one rule for 'unverified' catches none of the 118 the reviewer itself labelled. This plan asks a calibrated classifier about each leftover as the card is written and puts the few it scores as act-now in a short section at the top, with the rule and the full list untouched below. You read ten lines instead of eighty; after five runs the record says whether you acted on them, and the rollback is deleting the section.

**Goal:** An `experiment` under the floor (#1093, proposal 1–3; map #525): the sandbox boot asks TypeSafe's Jev five questions about every residual row as it writes the ledger — status and subject as two separate questions, then actor, attention and claim-falsity — records the answers beside the boot's own substring `kind`, and the pull request card gains one `Act on these` section above the `Residuals:` line for rows at attention ≥ 2.5, at most ten, absent when none qualify. The boot's rule stays and is the rollback (delete the card section); a failed call is one log line and the row and card are what they were; nothing — the gate, the merge, `routeToPlan` — reads a `jev` field. The reading is pre-registered in each task's Context and is never an exam.
**Closes:** #1093

**Tech Stack:** Bash (`fleet/sandbox-boot.sh`, its `python3 -c` readers, `curl` through the `fleet_curl` seam), Node ESM (the boot sims under `fleet/tests/test_sandbox_boot_*.mjs` on the rig `fleet/tests/_sandbox_boot_helpers.mjs`, bridged by `tests/test_fleet_suite.py`), the contract `fleet/CONTRACT.md`, the runbook `fleet/RUNBOOK.md`, the sim index `fleet/tests/README.md`. The classifier is reached over HTTPS as an exe.dev `http-proxy` integration; no SDK, no key in the repo.

**Spec:** none — #1093 is the signed input, its facts restated in each task's Context.

**Parallelization rationale:** two waves, widths 2 and 1. Wave 1: Task 1 (the triage — the TypeSafe seam, the per-row request, the `jev` field on the ledger rows, the request cache, the rig's stub arm, the new residuals sim) and Task 3 (the contract and runbook prose) share no file and no symbol. Wave 2: Task 2 (the card's `Act on these` section) `Consumes:` Task 1's `residual_rows()` — a chain, because Task 2's exam boots the script and reads the card, and a card row scored by Jev exists at runtime only once Task 1's seam has made the request and the rig's stub arm has answered it; the row shape alone, shared as a literal, cannot put a scored row on a card. Task 3 describes the section Task 2 renders, as sentences pinned in both Contexts, and calls nothing.
