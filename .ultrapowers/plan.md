# Every question put to Jev leaves a row, a census names the questions answered blind, and the supervisor is read a second time over what the worker did

**Grammar:** claims-v1

**Claim:** After a run I can open its record and see one row for every question put to Jev — which site asked, for which task and worker, the raw answers, how much state it was shown, how long it took and whether it answered — and one command over a range of runs tells me which questions sit near 0.5 or barely move within a role, with the runs it skipped printed beside its n. (elicited)
**Summary:** This makes every question the factory puts to Jev leave a line on the run's record, and adds a per-release reading of which questions Jev is answering blind. It exists because four of seven judgment sites could not be audited after the fact (n=4 runs, 193–196), and a question that hovers near 0.5 or gives every examiner the same score is being shown too little to judge. It lets you see a starved question on the record before any decision is allowed to depend on it; the supervisor's second reading over what the worker did is an experiment, recorded beside the old one and gating nothing, and its rollback is one switch in the policy file.

**Goal:** #1169, all three items: a `jev` row per call from the judge's one door (the fold's union reading moved behind it), a census script over evidence tags, and the supervisor's four questions asked a second time over observed work, record-only. One mechanical fix from #1198 rides the engine task: a file whose trimmed diff carries no hunk is not asked about.
**Closes:** #1169

**Tech Stack:** Node 24 ESM for the factory and its exams, no new npm dependency; `python3` standard library for the census and its pytest exam.
Spec: none on disk — issue #1169, its three comments of 2026-09-21 and #1198's test result are the brief, and everything a worker needs is in its Context. The sandbox holds no spec.

**Parallelization rationale:** one wave, width 4. No task consumes another's runtime behaviour: the judge task, the two engine tasks and the census share the `jev` row shape and the reader call shapes as literals in each Context; the engine-wiring exam hands `buildDeps` a fake `makeJudge` and never runs the real judge, the supervisor exam reads its pure module only, and the engine reads a judge reader that is absent as `null`. ### Task 1: The judge's one door writes a row per call, takes a `who`, and carries the union reading and the supervisor's second reader

**Claim:** I can see one row for every question put to Jev — which site asked, for which task and worker, the raw answers, how much state it was shown, how long it took and whether it answered. (derived)
Machine: M1. `makeJudge({ ask, emit, now, questionsPath, policyPath, log })`: a reader that calls `ask` calls `emit` exactly once for that call, with a row whose keys are exactly `kind`, `site`, `task`, `label`, `keys`, `values`, `state_bytes`, `ms`, `answered` — `kind` is `jev`, `keys` is `Object.keys` of the questions it sent, in order, `values` is the answers object exactly as `ask` resolved it, `state_bytes` is `Buffer.byteLength(JSON.stringify(state))` of the state it sent, `ms` is `now()` read after the call minus `now()` read before it (`now` defaults to `Date.now`), and `answered` is `true`.
M2. When `ask` resolves `null` or throws, the reader resolves what it resolves today and the one row carries `answered: false` and `values: null`.
M3. A reader's argument may carry `who: { task, label }`: the row's `task` and `label` are those values, or `null` when absent, and `who` is never a key of the `state` handed to `ask` — for `readNote`, `readAmendment` and `readSupervisor` the state is the argument without its `who` key and every other key unchanged.
M4. An `emit` that throws, or no `emit` at all, changes no reading and throws nothing; a reader that returns without calling `ask` (`readGuards` over an empty `tests`) emits no row.
M5. The row's `site` is the name the reader hands `askOnce` today (`readTask` → `task`, `readLanding` → `landing`), and a new reader `readSupervisorObserved(arg)` puts the `supervisor` set's four questions over its argument without `who`, resolves the flat answers as `readSupervisor` does, and emits site `supervisor.observed`.
M6. A new reader `readUnion({ hunks, who })` puts the `resolve` set's `independent_additions`, `shared_anchor` and `ordering_matters` questions over the state `{ hunks }` at site `resolve.union`, and resolves `{ union: true }` exactly when the first answer is at least `policy.resolve.union.independent_additions` and the other two are at most `shared_anchor_max` and `ordering_matters_max`, `{ union: false }` otherwise, and `null` when one of the three answers is missing.

### Task 3: One command over a range of runs names the questions Jev answers near 0.5 or near-constant within a role, and prints the runs it skipped beside its n

**Claim:** One command over a range of runs tells me which questions sit near 0.5 or barely move within a role, with the runs it skipped printed beside its n. (derived)
Machine: M1. `python3 skills/ultrapowers/scripts/jev_census.py <dir>…` reads every `run-<N>/events.jsonl` under the given directories and prints a tab-separated table whose header is `question`, `n`, `unanswered`, `in_band`, `share`, `mean`, `role_sd`, `flag`, one line per question sorted by `question` ascending, where `question` is `<site>/<key>` with the pairwise families folded (`M3__f1` → `M__f`, `M2__t0` → `M__t`, `g4` → `g`), `n` counts the numeric values read (a bare number or an answer's `noul`), `unanswered` counts rows with `answered: false` whose `keys` carry the question, `in_band` counts values `v` with `0.35 <= v <= 0.65`, `share` is `in_band / n`, `mean` is the mean, and `role_sd` is the largest population standard deviation over the roles holding at least two values — a role is the row's `label` up to its first `:`, or `-` when `label` is `null`; the three ratios print with two decimals, and `-` when there is nothing to compute.
M2. `flag` is `band` when `n >= 5` and `share >= 0.5`, `constant` when some role holds at least five values whose population standard deviation is at most `0.05`, `band,constant` when both, and `-` when neither.
M3. The last line is `runs: n=<count> read=<numbers, comma-separated> skipped=<entries, comma-separated, or none>`: a run directory whose `events.jsonl` carries no `jev` row is not counted and is listed as `<N>(no jev rows)`.
M4. With `--fetch <owner>/<repo> --runs <A>..<B> --into <dir>` (and `--gh <bin>`), it fetches each run's record off `ultra/evidence/run-<N>` with `catch_counter.py`'s own `fetch_runs`, reads the fetched directories, and lists a run whose `events.jsonl` did not answer as `<N>(no events.jsonl)`.
M5. It exits 0 on any readable input — a flag refuses nothing — and exits 2 with one line on stderr when `--fetch` lacks `--runs` or `--into`.
